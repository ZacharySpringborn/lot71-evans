Where We Stand at Day 99

Dean and Barbara, here is a straight read on how the listing is doing, what Terrace Park has sold for, and why lowering the price hasn’t changed much. Market data is from IRES MLS, pulled October 13, 2026.

Bottom Line

The price is down $7,000. Buyer activity hasn’t moved.

We’ve reduced four times, from $29,500 to $22,500 — about 24%. In that time one buyer has toured the home, and no one has requested a showing since August 31.

  • At $30.91 per sq ft we are already in line with the most recent Terrace Park sale ($29.41 / sq ft, $28,000 for a larger home).
  • We are the only home for sale in Terrace Park, so we aren’t being outpriced by a neighbor.
  • For a buyer, $1,015 a month in lot rent is a bigger number than our price cuts — see Section 6. Three paths forward are laid out in Section 7.
Activity

Seen, but rarely acted on

463 online views and 1 inquiry, but zero saves and zero shares. One buyer showing on August 31 (rated 2 of 5, “not interested”) and none in the 43 days since.

Price

Cuts haven’t produced showings

The one buyer showing happened at $27,000. Since then we’ve taken off another $4,500 across two reductions, and the calendar has stayed empty.

Supply

We’re the only listing in the park

No other Terrace Park home is for sale. Three 2-bed / 1-bath homes have sold here since March 2025, each lower than the one before: $60,000, $38,000, then $28,000.

Timing

Still inside the park’s normal time to sell

Those three sales took 111, 128, and 172 days. At 99 days we are slow, but not out of line for Terrace Park — a small buyer pool simply takes longer to find.

Option A · Hold
$22,500
No change · give it time
  • Already in line with the last park sale per square foot
  • Park sales have taken 111–172 days; we’re at 99
  • No competing listing in the park for a buyer to choose instead
  • Lot rent continues at $1,015 a month, and winter is typically slower
Option B · Change the Terms
Terms
Make it easier to buy, not just cheaper
  • Offer a lot-rent credit at closing (e.g., first 2–3 months, $2,030–$3,045) — aims straight at the monthly cost buyers weigh most
  • Consider seller financing to reach buyers who don’t have the full price in cash
  • Seller financing carries legal and lending-rule requirements; review with an attorney before offering
  • Lowers your net much like a price cut, but may change who can buy
Option C · Carpet & Paint Offer
Carpet & Paint
Instead of another price reduction
  • Seller covers new carpet and fresh interior paint for a qualified buyer under contract
  • Offered only to a buyer with verified proof of funds who is serious about closing
  • Work is ordered only once we’re confident the sale will close — never on a deal that could still fall apart
  • Paired with a small $500 price adjustment so price-change alerts go out to agents, saved searches, and Zillow
  • Costs a few thousand dollars, spent only on a sale that’s going to close — a price cut is given up up-front

Why Option C

The listing photos can give the impression that the home needs work before someone can move their things in. That usually isn’t a fair read, but it can stop a buyer before they ever schedule a showing. Promising new carpet and paint answers that objection directly, without lowering the price again.

Timing is flexible. We can do it now, or hold it in reserve. I can update the listing remarks anytime, but a remarks change alone doesn’t send any alerts. Only a price change notifies agents, saved searches, and Zillow / Realtor.com users who have already looked at the home. That’s the reason for the $500 adjustment.

How we’d protect you

  • Proof of funds required before the offer is extended or accepted
  • The carpet and paint commitment is written into the purchase contract, not just the listing
  • No work starts until the contract is past the points where it’s most likely to fall apart (for example, inspection and park approval)
  • If the contract ends before then, nothing is owed and nothing has been spent
  • Closing date set to leave time for the work to be completed

Final listing and contract wording to be confirmed before anything is published.

Context
Another price cut is still possible, but two reductions since the last showing haven’t produced a new one, and on a per-square-foot basis we’re already near the most recent sale in the park. Before we reduce the price again, I’d suggest we consider Option C.
